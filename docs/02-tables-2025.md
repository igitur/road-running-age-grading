# 02 — The 2025 tables

Source of truth for every number on this page: `data/mldr_road.json`, extracted
from the published calculator page, cross-checked against
`MLDRRoadFactors2025.xlsm` and `Male/FemaleRoadStd2025.xlsx`
([08-validation.md](08-validation.md)). Approved by the USATF MLDR Council on
**2025-01-10**; published workbooks dated **2025-07-27**.

## Shape of a table

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Sexes | 2 (separate male and female tables, never mixed) |
| Events (distances) | 22 |
| Ages | 96, every integer from **5 to 100** |
| Values per table | 22 × 96 = 2 112 factors, plus 22 open standards |
| Rows in the workbook | one row per event, one column per age |

Two facts follow immediately and drive everything in
[03-algorithm.md](03-algorithm.md):

1. Ages are **integers**. A fractional age is interpolated between the two
   neighbouring columns.
2. Only 22 distances exist. Anything else is interpolated between the two
   nearest listed distances.

Three distinct kinds of row, and the difference matters:

* **Fitted** (`1MileRoad`, `5 km`, `10 km`, half, marathon): a curve was fitted
  to observed single-age bests. The mile has no youth records, so its youth
  parameters were borrowed from the 5 km curve.
* **Interpolated** (6 km … 30 km): the *factor* for each age is interpolated in
  log-distance space between the two bracketing calibration distances
  ([04-methodology.md](04-methodology.md#4-distance-interpolation)).
* **Marathon curve reused** (50 km and beyond): the factors and the factor
  *shape* are identical to the marathon's. Only the open standard changes, so a
  longer race is graded purely by its open standard. Check it yourself:
  the male 50 km and 200 km factor rows are byte-identical to the marathon's.

## The open standards are not always the world record

At the four calibration distances the open standard is the world record. At the
other distances the record is **adjusted by hand** so that pace varies smoothly
with distance, and the adjustments are not always small (male values, from the
working file's `Parameters` sheet):

| Distance | Record in the sheet | Standard used | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 mile | 3:52 | 3:47 | −5 s (faster than the record) |
| 8 km | 22:02 | 20:55 | −67 s |
| 20 km | 56:01 | 54:20 | −101 s |
| 50 km | 2:43:38 | 2:27:00 | −16 min 38 s |
| Marathon | 2:00:35 | 2:00:35 | none (calibration) |

This is deliberate: a raw list of records produces a jagged pace-vs-distance
curve, and a jagged open standard would make age grades jump at one distance
compared with its neighbours. See
[04-methodology.md](04-methodology.md#2-open-standards).

## What a factor curve looks like

Male 10 km, selected ages (`f` from `data/mldr_road.json`):

| age | 5 | 7 | 10 | 12 | 15 | 17 | 19 | 21–30 | 35 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 80 | 90 | 100 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| f | .5073 | .6243 | .7698 | .8468 | .9323 | .9693 | .9903 | **1.0000** | .9909 | .9636 | .8886 | .8136 | .7386 | .6301 | .4546 | .2121 |

Shape: rising curve through childhood → flat plateau → gently falling line
through the 40s–60s → accelerating fall beyond ~70. That five-part shape is
exactly what the fitted model encodes
([04-methodology.md](04-methodology.md#3-the-seven-segment-curve)).

Female 10 km differs in two visible ways: the youth curve starts higher
(0.6850 at age 5 rather than 0.5073 — girls mature earlier relative to the
open standard) and the masters fall is slightly gentler until about 75, after
which it is steeper (0.1477 at 100 vs 0.2121 for men).

## Distance dependence of the factors

Same sex, same age, different distance — the factor moves with distance because
aging costs more over longer races:

| age 60 | 1 mile | 5 km | 10 km | half | marathon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| male | .7901 | .8075 | .8136 | .8082 | .8143 |
| female | .8014 | .7621 | .7828 | .7718 | .7965 |

Nothing here is monotonic in distance: for men the half marathon (.8082) is
*harsher* than both the 10 km and the marathon; for women the mile (.8014) is
gentler than the 5 km, 10 km and half. The curves are fitted independently per
distance, so do not extrapolate a rule from one row to another.

## Worked examples

All produced by `brrag` and reproducible with `tools/verify.py`.

```console
$ brrag 40:00 --age 40 --sex M --distance 10k --show-standard
open standard  26:24
age standard   27:23.8  (scores 100.0%)
factor         0.9636
age-graded     38:32.6
age grade        68.49%
```

```console
$ brrag 40:00 --age 56 --sex F --distance 10k
factor         0.8226
age-graded     32:54.2
age grade        87.42%
```

Same time, same distance, 16 years apart and a different sex: 68.49% vs 87.42%.
That is the entire point of the system.

```console
$ brrag 4:30:00 --age 75 --sex M --distance marathon --show-standard
open standard  2:00:35
age standard   2:55:51  (scores 100.0%)
factor         0.6857
age-graded     3:05:08
age grade        65.13%
```

```console
$ brrag 22:00 --age 12 --sex F --distance 5k
factor         0.8827
age-graded     19:25.2
age grade        71.58%
```

## Precision as published

* Factors are published to **4 decimal places**; that is all the precision that
  exists. Do not report percentages to more than 0.01 pp or factors beyond
  4 dp — the underlying numbers do not support it.
* The published age standards (seconds) are rounded to **whole seconds** in the
  `AgeStdSec` sheets; the factor sheet keeps 4 dp. The two therefore disagree by
  up to ±0.5 s per cell by design.
* `data/mldr_road.json` keeps every value exactly as published — no
  re-rounding, no smoothing.
